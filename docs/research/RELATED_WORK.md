## Verified limitations and overlap

ALPS is a verified overlap with the proposed user-space-control-plane plus learned scheduling direction: its frontend learns scheduling policies in userspace and applies them through eBPF to Linux scheduling. Its evaluation is specifically serverless function scheduling, using OpenLambda on a 24-core Ubuntu host and production Huawei/Azure traces; it does not establish benefit for a desktop's heterogeneous inference-cost decision. The paper explicitly reports that it uses SRPT simulation to train and says its simulation cannot fully capture workload shifts/context-switch costs. Its reported end-to-end turnaround improvement is a measured workload result; the SRPT simulation is not an additional hardware measurement. Powercap documentation further cautions against treating platform domain counters as whole-machine energy by default.

## HARM 的定位（不主张首创性）

HARM does not claim novelty for a user-space control plane (ghOSt/sched_ext family; ALPS directly overlaps by verified evidence), prediction-assisted scheduling (ALPS; LAKE remains unverified here), or cost-aware prediction skipping (SkipPredict; metareasoning's primary text was not opened in this check). These similarities are overlap, not proof of equivalence. The smallest defensible research question is: **On a user-delegated Linux cgroup, does a controller that predicts only when a conservative estimate of allocation benefit exceeds measured observation/inference/actuation cost improve paired end-to-end net benefit over native allocation and a predict-always policy, without breaching pre-registered latency/safety bounds?** Test with matched native / always-predict / cost-gated trials, charge CPU time and latency to each acquisition/inference/actuation term, capture PSI and cgroup outcomes, and report energy only if full-machine measurement is available. This is a question for an experiment, not a novelty or outcome claim.
